About Me

I am a PhD student in Thayer Engineering at Dartmouth College, advised by Prof. Bryce L. Ferguson. I study optimization and decision-making under uncertainty. My current research asks how routing recommendations can remain incentive-compatible when users hold different prior beliefs.

I received my M.S. in Applied Statistics from the University of Michigan and my B.Sc. in Statistics from Zhongnan University of Economics and Law. At Michigan, I worked with Prof. Max Z. Li on air traffic management, Prof. Lee F. Schroeder on diagnostic network optimization, Prof. Donglin Zeng on longitudinal health data, and Prof. Yuekai Sun on synthetic control.

Robust information design. I analyze obedience guarantees and the cost of robustness to differences in beliefs.
Optimization and computation. I derive finite-grid approximation guarantees and implement sparse linear and conic optimization models.
Statistical modeling. My previous work includes longitudinal data analysis and synthetic control.
